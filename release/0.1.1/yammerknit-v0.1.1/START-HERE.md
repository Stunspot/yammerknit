# Start here: build a thoughtbase you can think with

Yammerknit turns source material into reusable cognitive architecture. A thoughtbase carries ways to compare, reframe and reason, together with the source evidence and its limits. Begin with [installation](docs/INSTALLATION.md), or follow [the complete worked example](codex-plugin/yammerknit/skills/yammerknit/examples/README.md) from one source to a thoughtbase and then into a different problem.

After installation, supply your material and ask: “Build a Yammerknit thoughtbase from this source. Follow its conceptual gravity, construct the keystone bridges, and give me reasoning seeds I can use elsewhere.” Add an intended use when you have one. Name a workspace only when you want durable records.

Open a cluster, follow its bridge and try the linked reasoning seed on a new question. The useful result is a thought structure you can work with; a recap or a row of topic labels is insufficient. Keep source claims, interpretations and proposed transfers distinguishable as you explore.

Follow [create, save, browse and return](docs/USE-YAMMERKNIT.md) for the working journey. Use [data and portability](docs/DATA-AND-PRIVACY.md) to move the complete record, [recovery](docs/TROUBLESHOOTING.md) for a failure, and [validation and limits](docs/VALIDATION-AND-LIMITS.md) to interpret a pass or reproduce the kit. The separate retrieval system performs any embedding and ingestion; saving a thoughtbase does not perform those operations.
